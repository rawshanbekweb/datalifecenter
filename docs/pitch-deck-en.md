## Slide 2 — The Problem

**Talent exists in the regions. The infrastructure does not.**

- Uzbekistan's IT growth is concentrated in Tashkent; regions like Karakalpakstan are served last.
- Local courses are mostly theory — graduates finish with a certificate but no shipped work.
- Learning material in Uzbek is limited. In **Karakalpak, it is almost non-existent.**
- Students pay first and only then discover the group is overcrowded and the mentor is unreachable.
- Employers cannot verify whether a certificate means anything.

*Speaker notes:* This is not a demand problem — we have had 3,000+ people come through our doors. It is a delivery problem. The bottleneck is quality, language, and trust, not interest.

## Slide 6 — How It Works

**Six steps, all visible to the student from day one.**

1. **Apply** — pick a track on the site and leave a request.
2. **Enroll at the center** — payment is handled by an administrator; the course unlocks automatically in the student's cabinet. No money moves through the website.
3. **Study the program** — lessons in the personal cabinet, progress calculated automatically.
4. **Get real feedback** — mentors set assignments, review submissions, return them for rework, and grade.
5. **Ask questions where they arise** — Q&A sits under each lesson; the student is notified when a mentor answers.
6. **Finish with a verifiable certificate** — each PDF carries a public verification link an employer can check.

**Small groups. Seats fill, enrollment closes, a waitlist opens — no hidden conditions.**

*Speaker notes:* Two deliberate choices judges usually ask about. First: no online payments — in our market trust is built face to face, and it removes fraud and chargeback risk entirely. Second: capped groups — we cap revenue per cohort on purpose, because the reputation of a regional school is its only marketing channel.

## Slide 8 — Business Model

**Four revenue lines, one team.**

1. **Tuition** — 600,000–1,000,000 UZS per course, 2–6 months, online or offline.
2. **Client software delivery** — web, mobile, UI/UX, MVPs for startups; the highest-margin line and the source of teaching material.
3. **Corporate training** — closed groups for a company's staff, curriculum adapted to their actual workload.
4. **Institutional partnerships** — internships and joint programs with universities and colleges; partner branding on datalife.uz.

**Why it holds:** the studio smooths seasonality in enrollment, and top graduates join delivery — so growth does not require proportional senior hiring.

*Speaker notes:* If you want one number to test us on, it's cost of acquiring a student versus lifetime value across the ladder — a computer-literacy student who comes back for Python and then Frontend pays us three times.

## Slide 9 — Technology

**Built in-house, engineered like a product — not a website.**

- **Stack:** React 19 + TypeScript, Node.js + PostgreSQL, cloud file storage
- **118 automated tests** run on every change before it is deployed
- **Role-based access:** separate cabinets and permissions for student, mentor, admin

*Speaker notes:* We show this slide because it is the difference between a course business and a technology company. The platform is an asset we own, test, and can license.
